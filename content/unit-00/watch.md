# Watch out

Notes from Phineas's kit on things that are easy to mix up or where the slides need care. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 1) Behavioral vs. cognitive: behavioral = rewards and learned behavior; cognitive = thoughts (like changing how you think about something).
- (deck 1) Humanistic vs. psychodynamic: humanistic looks forward (potential, positivity, relationships); psychodynamic looks back (unconscious mind, childhood).
- (deck 1) The "Identify the perspective" slide has blank answers. The practice questions here use the best-fit answer for each; check them against what your teacher went over in class.
- (deck 2) Your Dependent Variable slide labels its answers "IV = strength of memory" and "IV = test scores." Those are DVs; the slide has a typo. IV = what the researcher changes. DV = what gets measured.
- (deck 2) The positive and negative correlation slides are titled +1.0 and -1.0. Those are perfect correlations; real correlations can be anywhere from -1 to +1.
- (deck 2) Only experiments can show cause and effect. Correlation does not equal causation.
- (deck 2) Informed consent comes BEFORE the study. Debriefing comes AFTER.
- (deck 3) The mean always follows the tail: positive skew → the mean is highest; negative skew → the mean is lowest.
- (deck 3) Your slides call percentile rank a "fourth measure of central tendency." Many textbooks list it separately, but go with your slides.
- (deck 3) Descriptive statistics organize and describe data (mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation). Inferential statistics decide whether results apply to a whole population (p-values, significance).
