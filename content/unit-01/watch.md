# Watch out

Notes from Phineas's kit on things that are easy to mix up or where the slides need care. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 2) Excitatory vs. inhibitory list from your slides — Excitatory: glutamate (major), ACh, norepinephrine, substance P. Inhibitory: GABA (major), dopamine, serotonin, endorphins.
- (deck 2) Your slide spells norepinephrine's hormone name "non-adrenaline." The real word is noradrenaline. Also, many textbooks say dopamine can act both ways; for your test, go with your teacher's list.
- (deck 3) The melatonin slide says the pineal gland is "in the thalamus." It actually sits near the thalamus, deep in the brain.
- (deck 4) F-POT order front to back along the top: Frontal, Parietal, Occipital — with Temporal on the sides.
- (deck 4) Scans: structure only = CT, MRI. Activity/function = PET, fMRI, EEG.
- (deck 6) The vestibular slide says the vestibular sacs are "of the cochlea." They sit right next to the cochlea in the inner ear but aren't part of it.
- (deck 6) Parts of the ear and eye come from diagram slides with no text. If your test has a labeling question, look at those slides directly.
- (deck 7) Your slides list the four sleep stages by name only (the details were in the video). The one stage fact on the slides: night terrors happen in Stage 3.
- (deck 7) Ecstasy/MDMA shows up twice on your slides: as a form of amphetamine (stimulant) and as a hallucinogen. It counts as both.
- (deck 7) Alcohol is a depressant, even though people think it's a stimulant because of the crazy things people do when drinking.
- (deck 8) Two-step trick: (1) the visual field goes to the opposite hemisphere, (2) that hemisphere controls the opposite hand. Only the left hemisphere can say what it saw.
- (deck 8) The slide on the left visual field says the patient can draw or point "with the right hand." Its own picture shows the left hand drawing, and the left hand is correct: the left visual field reaches only the right hemisphere, which controls the left hand.
